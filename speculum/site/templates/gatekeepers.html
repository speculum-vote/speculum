The gatekeepers

Most bills that organizations lobby on never reach a vote. Committees decide which bills move. This page compares what organizations in an industry gave to members of the committee that writes that industry's laws with what they gave to every other member of the same chamber.

What this shows, and what it doesn't

  • It shows where an industry's money goes. It doesn't show a vote bought or a motive. Members often seek the committee that matters to their district: farm-state members sit on Agriculture, and the money of their home industry may have come first.
  • Labor unions are the exception. Their gifts to the committees over labor law are close to their gifts to everyone else, because unions give broadly across their own side.
  • Sectors are read from each organization's own description of its business, the rule used on the Bills page. Lobbying firms serve many industries, so their gifts count only as other money. {{ g.known_share|pct }} of the {{ g.total|money }} reported could be placed in a sector.
  • Committee seats are current membership (public-domain data from congress-legislators). A member who changed committees during the two years is counted where they sit now. Gifts made in a member's honor, such as to an event or a leadership PAC, are counted as LD-203 reports them.
