The rule

  • Lobbied: the organization named the bill on a quarterly lobbying report (LD-2) and files its own reports. Lobbying firms are left out, since a firm works for many clients.
  • The vote: in each chamber, the last roll call that decided the measure itself: passage, adoption, agreeing to the other chamber's amendment or a conference report, or passage over a veto. Votes on rules, motions, amendments and cloture are not the decision. Simple House and Senate resolutions are left out.
  • Funded: federal campaign contributions the organization reported on LD-203, by it or its PAC, to a member who voted yea or nay on that vote. Every contribution reported for {{ years }} counts, before or after the vote. Contributions a lobbyist reports as their own are not counted.
  • Both sides: the organization funded at least one member who voted yes and at least one who voted no.
  • A bill qualifies once at least {{ min_funders }} organizations that lobbied it funded members who voted on it.
Funding members on both sides of a vote is common and lawful, and has many explanations: an organization may give to the members who lead its committees, to both parties as a matter of policy, or for reasons unrelated to this bill. This page records the pattern and leaves the reading to you.
